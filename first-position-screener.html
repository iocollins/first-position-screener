When to go in, when to come out

Honestly: no one — including professional fund managers — reliably calls short-term tops and bottoms. What works instead is deciding your rules before you're emotional.

01

Go in on a schedule, not a hunch

Split your starting amount into equal chunks and invest on a fixed calendar (e.g. monthly) regardless of price. This is dollar-cost averaging — it removes the "is now a good time?" question entirely and is the standard first move for a beginner.

02

Set a rebalancing band before you need one

Pick target weights (e.g. 70% broad equity / 20% bonds / 10% dividend). If a position drifts more than ~5 percentage points from its target, trim it back — that's your disciplined "sell some." It's mechanical, not a prediction.

03

Use valuation as a caution light, not a signal

When broad-market valuation (P/E relative to its own long-run average) is stretched well above normal, that's a reason to keep contributing on schedule but not accelerate — not a reason to sell everything.

04

Write your exit rule while you're calm

Decide in advance what would actually change your mind about a holding — a thesis breaking, not a red day. A rule written after a 20% drop is usually panic wearing a strategy's clothes.
